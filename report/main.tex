\begin{abstract}
The particle filter carries inference on a graphical model from discrete variables to continuous, non-Gaussian
ones by representing each belief with weighted samples. How well it works depends on its sampling choices: how
new particles are proposed, how and when they are resampled, and how many there are. This report writes robot
localization and simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) as dynamic Bayesian networks, derives the particle
filter and FastSLAM, the Rao-Blackwellized particle filter that samples the robot's trajectory and estimates each
landmark analytically, as approximate inference on them, and compares these choices in controlled experiments
over 20 simulated worlds each. The four common resampling schemes give the same accuracy in localization, while
systematic resampling keeps the most distinct particles; resampling adaptively, when the effective sample size
or the largest weight signals degeneracy, is as accurate as resampling at every step, with low thresholds resampling at
only a third to a half of the steps, and never resampling triples the error. Measurement-informed proposals keep a larger effective sample
size: the auxiliary particle filter is the most accurate localization filter from 100 particles on, and
FastSLAM 2.0 gives a better map than FastSLAM 1.0 with 5 to 10 particles. Beyond about 500 particles the error
of the bootstrap filter improves only slowly while the runtime keeps growing.
\end{abstract}
